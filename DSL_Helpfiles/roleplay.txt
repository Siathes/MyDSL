        Roleplay

    Roleplaying is required in DSL. However, don't think of it as a chore.
      Tips for enjoying role play is to pick a race/class that you like.
      If you like being a rash bully, play a bugbear barbarian. If you
      want to be a humble aide, play a human cleric. If you want to
      intelligent, but vile criminal, try a dark elven thief.

    Whatever you pick, do some research on the race and class. Most of
      them have traits that are critical to their society. Tinker gnomes,
      for example, all have a life quest in which they will spend their
      entire lives researching one topic.
    Kender are fearless, good-hearted adventurers. Wemics are still
      primitive and aren't completely in tune with society. Incorporate
      these traits into your roleplay.

    Characters are a lot more interesting to play if you work them from
      the ground up. Don't enter the world automatically demanding that
      everyone see you as some superhero. Start as that incompetent
      student, poor waiter, or homeless musician and change your character
      according to interactions. It'll be more enjoyable to look over
      your story of rags-to-riches.

    Finally, make your character have faults. Everyone has them, they're
      definitive to our personalities. Maybe your mage is so obsessed
      with his research that he fails to observe the world around him.
      Maybe your half-elf has trouble confronting people. Faults make
      conflict, and conflict is what's fun to read and write about.
      However, keep faults within sensible bounds: kender are not greedy
      thieves or murderous, goblins do not want to help the greater
      good, and Shalonesti elves are not naive and childish.
